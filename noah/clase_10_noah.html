1

Ir a + infinitivo

12 min

To talk about a plan or intention — something you've decided is going to happen — Spanish almost always reaches for ir a + infinitivo, not a separate future tense. It works exactly like English "going to": conjugate ir, add a, then the infinitive doesn't change at all.

Ir (present) + a

the only part that conjugates

  • yoVoy a visitar a Sofía en Sevilla.
  • tú¿Vas a venir a la boda?
  • él/ellaManolo va a contar la historia otra vez.
  • nosotrosVamos a mudarnos el año que viene.

+ infinitivo

never changes form

  • -arVoy a hablar con su padre.
  • -erVamos a comer en la caseta otra vez.
  • -ir¿Vais a vivir en Sevilla o en Manchester?
  • ellosSofía y su prima van a organizar la boda.
Pattern to notice: only ir conjugates (voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van) — the infinitive after it is fixed. That makes ir a + infinitivo the easiest way to talk about the future in Spanish, and the one people actually use in conversation.

A few key futures, and time markers

10 min

You'll also hear the simple future (será, tendremos, iremos) — it's more formal or definite than ir a, and useful for a handful of common irregular verbs. Then the words that place a plan in time.

Three simple-future forms worth knowing

Ser → será

Used for predictions and things that feel more fixed, like a wedding date.

La boda será en junio. = The wedding will be in June.

Tener → tendremos

Irregular stem (tendr-), then the normal future endings — common for "we'll have".

Tendremos una fiesta grande. = We'll have a big party.

Ir → iremos

Regular future endings on the infinitive — no stem change here.

Iremos todos juntos a la playa. = We'll all go to the beach together.
Ir a + infinitivo vs simple future: for everyday plans, use ir a + infinitivo (Voy a mudarme en verano) — it's what people actually say. Save será/tendremos/iremos for predictions, promises, or slightly more formal moments, like Manolo announcing something at dinner: El año que viene será diferente para todos.
