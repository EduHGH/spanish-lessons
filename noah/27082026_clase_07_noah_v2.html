The pretérito indefinido

12 min

Until now you've talked in the present — trabajo, vivo, me gusta. Today's new tense lets you talk about things that are finished: what you did yesterday, last week, or at the Feria. It's the tense of finished stories.

-AR · bailar

to dance

  • yobailé toda la noche.
  • túbailaste muy bien.
  • él/ellaSofía bailó sevillanas.
  • nosotrosBailamos hasta tarde.

-ER/-IR · comer, salir

to eat, to go out

  • yocomí pescaíto frito.
  • tú¿Saliste con Sofía?
  • él/ellaManolo comió mucho.
  • nosotrosSalimos a la caseta.
Pattern to notice: -ER and -IR verbs share the same endings in the pretérito (comí/salí, comiste/saliste, comió/salió) — one less thing to learn separately, unlike in the present.

2

Four irregulars you'll use constantly

10 min

These four break the regular pattern — but they're also the most useful verbs for telling a story, so it's worth learning them properly from day one.

Irregular preterite stems

Ir / ser → fui, fuiste, fue…

Ir (to go) and ser (to be) share the exact same forms in the preterite — context tells you which one is meant.

Fui a la Feria. = I went to the Feria.

Tener → tuve, tuviste, tuvo…

Not "tené" — the stem changes completely, then takes irregular endings without accents.

Tuve mucha suerte. = I got lucky / had a lot of luck.

Hacer → hice, hiciste, hizo…

Watch the "yo" form: hice (with c), but hizo in "él/ella" (with z) — the sound stays the same, the spelling shifts.

¿Qué hiciste ayer? = What did you do yesterday?

Estar → estuve, estuviste, estuvo…

Same irregular pattern as tener — estuv- instead of estar-, then the same endings.

Estuve en la caseta. = I was at the marquee.
Common trap for English speakers: don't add "-í" to irregular stems (tuví, hací) — once the stem changes (tuv-, hic-, estuv-), the endings lose their accents: tuve, no tuví.
