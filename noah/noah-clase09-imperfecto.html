The imperfecto

12 min

The pretérito (fui, comí, estuve) is for events that happened once and finished. The imperfecto is for the background: what things were like, what used to happen again and again, ongoing states with no clear end. In English it's often "used to" or "was/were -ing".

-AR · hablar

to speak

  • yoDe niño hablaba muy poco español. As a boy I spoke very little Spanish.
  • tú¿Hablabas otro idioma en casa? Did you speak another language at home?
  • él/ellaMi abuela hablaba con acento del norte. My grandmother spoke with a northern accent.
  • nosotrosHablábamos inglés todos los días. We used to speak English every day.

-ER/-IR · comer, vivir

to eat, to live

  • yoDe pequeño comía mucho pescado. As a child I used to eat a lot of fish.
  • tú¿Dónde vivías antes? Where did you use to live before?
  • él/ellaMi familia vivía cerca del mar. My family used to live near the sea.
  • nosotrosVivíamos en un pueblo pequeño. We used to live in a small town.
Pattern to notice: the imperfecto is almost completely regular — -ER and -IR verbs share the same endings (comía/vivía, comías/vivías), and only three verbs are irregular in the whole language: ser, ir and ver.

Three irregulars, and pretérito vs imperfecto

10 min

Only ser, ir and ver break the pattern — learn these three and the imperfecto is done. Then the real skill: knowing which past tense to reach for.

The only three irregular imperfectos

Ser → era, eras, era…

No stem change at all in the sense of "irregular endings" — it's just short and doesn't follow -ar/-er patterns.

De niño era muy tímido. = As a child I was very shy.

Ir → iba, ibas, iba…

Used constantly for habitual trips: where you used to go, again and again.

Íbamos a la playa cada verano. = We used to go to the beach every summer.

Ver → veía, veías, veía…

Keeps the -e- from the infinitive all the way through, unlike a regular -er verb.

Veía mucho a mis abuelos. = I used to see my grandparents a lot.
Pretérito vs imperfecto: use pretérito for a single finished event (Ayer fui a Sevilla — Yesterday I went to Seville) and imperfecto for background, description or repeated habit (Cuando era pequeño, iba a Sevilla cada verano — When I was little, I used to go to Seville every summer). Often they appear in the same sentence: Mientras cenábamos, sonó el teléfono — While we were having dinner, the phone rang — imperfecto for what was going on, pretérito for the single event that interrupted it.
