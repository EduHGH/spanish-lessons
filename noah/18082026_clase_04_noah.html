Querer and poder

10 min

You already know quiero and puedo from Class 3. Today we use them for one specific job: ordering politely at a bar or restaurant. Both verbs are irregular in the "yo" and "tú" forms — the pattern below is the one you'll use most.

yo (I)quieroQuiero una cerveza.
tú (you)quieres¿Qué quieres tú?
él / ella (he/she)quiereSofía quiere agua con gas.
yo (I)puedo¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?
tú (you)puedes¿Puedes traerme la cuenta?
usted (you, formal)puede¿Me puede traer la carta?
The magic phrase for waiters: "¿Me puede traer...?" (Can you bring me...?) is the single most useful sentence for ordering. It's polite, formal, and works with anything — food, drinks, or the bill.

Direct order vs polite request — both are fine, but sound different

QUIERO — simple, common at a bar
  • Ordering food: Quiero un plato de jamón.
  • Ordering drinks: Quiero una copa de vino tinto.
  • Note: Perfectly polite in Spain, especially at a busy tapas bar. Add "por favor" and it's friendly, not rude.
¿ME PUEDE...? — extra polite, sit-down restaurant
  • Asking for the menu: ¿Me puede traer la carta?
  • Asking for the bill: ¿Me puede traer la cuenta?
  • Asking permission: ¿Puedo sentarme aquí?
Watch out — a common trap for English speakers: Spanish waiters aren't offended by "Quiero..." the way an English "I want..." might sound blunt. In Sevilla you'll hear locals say "Quiero una caña" constantly — it's completely normal. Save "¿Me puede traer...?" for when you want to sound a bit more formal, like asking for the bill.

El / la, un / una — gender

8 min

Every Spanish noun is either masculine or feminine — there's no logical reason, you just learn each word with its article. El/un = masculine. La/una = feminine. On a menu, this decides whether you say "un" or "una".

EL / UN — masculine
  • el plato — un plato de jamón
  • el vino — un vino tinto
  • el agua* — un agua con gas *feminine word, but uses "el/un" because it starts with stressed "a"
  • el postre — un postre de la casa
LA / UNA — feminine
  • la carta — la carta, por favor
  • la cuenta — la cuenta, por favor
  • la cerveza — una cerveza bien fría
  • la tapa — una tapa de tortilla
Quick trick: most words ending in -o are masculine (el vino, el plato) and most ending in -a are feminine (la carta, la cerveza) — but there are exceptions, so it's worth learning menu words with their article from day one.

Numbers 0–100 for prices

6 min

You need numbers to understand prices and pay the bill. Here are the key building blocks — once you know these, you can build almost any price between 0 and 100.

0 cero 1 uno 2 dos 3 tres 4 cuatro 5 cinco 6 seis 7 siete 8 ocho 9 nueve 10 diez 15 quince 20 veinte 30 treinta 40 cuarenta 50 cincuenta 60 sesenta 100 cien
Building bigger numbers: from 31 upward, you join with "y" — treinta y dos (32), cuarenta y cinco (45). Prices with cents work the same way: son doce euros con cincuenta (that's €12.50).
