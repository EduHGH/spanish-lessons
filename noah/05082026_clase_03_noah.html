Gustar, querer and poder

11 min

Gustar doesn't work like normal verbs — it works backwards from English. Instead of "I like the beach", Spanish literally says "the beach pleases me". Querer and poder work like normal verbs and are followed by another verb in its infinitive form.

a mí (to me)me gustaMe gusta la playa.
a ti (to you)te gusta¿Te gusta el flamenco?
a él/ella (to him/her)le gustaA Sofía le gusta la playa.
a nosotros (to us)nos gustaNos gusta la música.
a vosotros (to you all)os gusta¿Os gusta el golf?
a ellos (to them)les gustaLes gusta la guitarra.
Key rule: use gusta with one thing you like (me gusta la playa) and gustan with more than one (me gustan las guitarras). The verb agrees with the thing being liked, not with the person.

Querer and poder + infinitive

yo (I)quiero / puedoQuiero tocar la guitarra. Puedo jugar al golf hoy.
tú (you)quieres / puedes¿Quieres ir a la playa?
él / ella (he/she)quiere / puedeSofía quiere escuchar flamenco.
nosotros/as (we)queremos / podemosQueremos viajar a la playa este finde.
vosotros/as (you all)queréis / podéis¿Podéis venir al concierto?
ellos/as (they)quieren / puedenQuieren jugar al golf el sábado.
Querer (to want) and poder (to be able to / can) are both followed directly by an infinitive verb: quiero tocar, puedo jugar, queremos viajar. No extra word needed — just verb + infinitive.

Gusta vs gustan — one thing or several?

GUSTA — one thing / an action
  • Singular noun: Me gusta la playa. One thing being liked.
  • An activity: Me gusta jugar al golf. Verbs after "gustar" always count as singular.
GUSTAN — more than one thing
  • Plural noun: Me gustan las guitarras. More than one thing being liked.
  • Several items: Me gustan los restaurantes de Sevilla.
Watch out — a common trap for English speakers: in English "I like" always uses "I" as the subject, but in Spanish the thing you like is the subject: "me gusta LA PLAYA" (the beach pleases me), not "yo gusto la playa". Don't translate word-for-word — think "it pleases me" instead of "I like it".
