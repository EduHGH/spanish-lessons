1

Direct and indirect object pronouns

12 min

Direct object pronouns (lo, la, los, las) replace the thing being given, told, or done. Indirect object pronouns (le, les) replace the person receiving it. Both go right before the conjugated verb.

Direct: lo, la, los, las

replaces the thing

  • el regaloLo compré ayer.
  • la noticiaLa conté en la cena.
  • los anillosLos tiene Manolo.
  • las fotosLas mandé a mi madre.

Indirect: le, les

replaces the person

  • a SofíaLe di un regalo.
  • a su padreLe conté la noticia.
  • a sus padresLes escribí una carta.
  • a mis amigosLes voy a decir la fecha.
Pattern to notice: le/les never change for gender — only lo/la/los/las do, because those track the thing, not the person. Also, le and les often show up even when the person is also named: Le di el regalo a Sofía — that's normal, not a mistake.

2

Combining both: se lo di

10 min

When both pronouns appear together — the thing AND the person — indirect comes first, then direct. But le/les becomes se right before lo/la/los/las. That's the one irregular twist to learn.

Le/les → se, before lo/la/los/las

Le + lo → se lo

"I gave it to her/him" — le becomes se because lo follows.

Se lo di a ella. = I gave it to her.

Le + la → se la

Same swap, this time with a feminine direct object.

Se la conté anoche. = I told it (the news) to him/her last night.

Les + los → se los

Works the same with plural people and plural things.

Se los di a mis padres. = I gave them (the rings) to my parents.
¿Se lo dijiste?: the same swap happens in questions and with any conjugated verb — decir, contar, dar, regalar all work this way. ¿Se lo dijiste a Manolo? "Did you tell it to Manolo?" Never "le lo dijiste" — Spanish always blocks that combination and forces se lo.
