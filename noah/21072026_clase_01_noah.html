The verb ser

10 min

Ser means "to be". We use it for things that don't change from day to day: your name, your nationality, your job, your personality.

yo (I)soySoy Noah.
tú (you)eresEres muy simpática.
él / ella (he/she)esMi novia es de Sevilla.
nosotros/as (we)somosSomos una pareja feliz.
vosotros/as (you all)soisSois muy amables.
ellos/as (they)sonSon los amigos de mi novia.
We use "ser" to say who you are in a permanent way: your name, where you're from, your job, and your personality. It doesn't change from one day to the next.

Ser or estar? Today we only work with "ser" — here's how to recognise it

SER — identity, today
  • Name: Soy Noah.
  • Nationality / origin: Soy inglés. Mi novia es de Sevilla.
  • Job: Soy agente de viajes.
  • Personality (how you always are): Eres muy simpática. It doesn't change tomorrow or the day after.
  • Relationship between people: Es mi novia. Son los amigos de mi novia.
ESTAR — next class
  • Location: Estoy en Sevilla. Where you are right now.
  • Mood or physical state (something temporary): Estoy nervioso hoy. It can change tomorrow.
  • Temporary situation: El bar está abierto.
Watch out — a common trap for English speakers: in English there is only one verb "to be" for all of this, but in Spanish the key question is: is it something permanent/about identity (name, nationality, job, character) → ser, or is it temporary/about location (mood, location) → estar? "Soy nervioso" (a character trait, always true) is not the same as "Estoy nervioso" (right now, today).
